What Are Hydrogen Fuel Cells?

Clean energy devices that generate electricity through chemical reactions, not combustion

Chemical Electricity Generator

A hydrogen fuel cell is a device that generates electricity through a chemical reaction, not combustion. It combines hydrogen (H₂) and oxygen (O₂) to produce electricity, heat, and water.

The Basic Equation

$$2H_2 + O_2 \rightarrow 2H_2O + \text{electricity + heat}$$

Two hydrogen molecules combine with one oxygen molecule to produce two water molecules, electricity, and heat

1

Anode (negative electrode)

  • Hydrogen gas enters the anode
  • A catalyst (usually platinum) splits hydrogen molecules into protons (H⁺) and electrons (e⁻)
$H_2 \rightarrow 2H^+ + 2e^-$
2

Electrolyte (proton exchange membrane, PEM)

  • Only protons can pass through the membrane
  • Electrons are forced to travel through an external circuit, creating an electric current
3

Cathode (positive electrode)

  • Oxygen gas enters the cathode
  • Protons, electrons, and oxygen combine to form water (H₂O) as the only emission
$\frac{1}{2}O_2 + 2H^+ + 2e^- \rightarrow H_2O$

Current Challenges

Obstacles we're working to overcome in hydrogen technology

Hydrogen Production

Issue: Often comes from natural gas (not fully clean)

Solution: Green hydrogen from electrolysis using renewable energy is developing but still costly

Progress: 35%

Storage & Transport

Issue: Hydrogen is light, needs compression or liquefaction

Impact: Energy-intensive storage and transportation requirements

Progress: 45%

Infrastructure

Issue: Few hydrogen fueling stations compared to EV charging

Need: Widespread infrastructure development for mass adoption

Progress: 25%

Cost

Issue: Platinum catalysts and fuel cell systems are expensive

Trend: Costs decreasing with scale and technology improvements

Progress: 40%

H2GP Racing Application

How we use hydrogen fuel cells in competitive racing

In the Hydrogen Grand Prix (H2GP), fuel cells provide the primary power source for our race cars, demonstrating the practical applications of this clean technology in high-performance transportation.

Racing Advantages:

  • Consistent Power Output: Steady electrical supply throughout the race
  • Lightweight Design: More energy-dense than traditional batteries
  • Silent Operation: Minimal noise compared to combustion engines
  • Educational Value: Students learn clean energy while experiencing competitive racing
